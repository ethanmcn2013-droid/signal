_Apple Product Review — Keynote Readiness Pass_

# Signal Studio — Product Review

**Date:** 2026-07-05
**Reviewer stance:** Apple internal product review, keynote-readiness bar.
**Scope:** every shipped surface — marketing homepage and content pages, the in-app briefing, onboarding, settings, account, all loading/empty/error/success states, chrome, and every string of copy. Read-only pass; no files changed.
**Bar:** the product's own mandate — _"A briefing, not a dashboard. Three things in plain English. Silence is the signal. Plain language, no dashboard charts, no marketing of automation as AI."_

---

## The one-paragraph verdict

Signal is one of the most _disciplined_ products I have reviewed. The engine is honest (no model in the path, hard caps, deterministic prose), the quiet-day all-clear is genuinely beautiful, the loading system has a real philosophy, and the writing — when it holds — is the best argument the product makes. But it is not keynote-ready, and the reason is singular and damning: **the product cannot agree with itself on what it is.** The core promise — "three things" — is told to the visitor as **one**, as **three**, as **four**, and as **six**, sometimes on the same screen. The artifact is named three different things and fires at three different times. Half the trust pages contradict the other half on whether a machine writes the words, when your data is deleted, and whether an LLM is coming. And in dark mode — which the codebase fully supports and a user can trigger — two live surfaces render as white slabs. A keynote demo is a promise made at 60fps to millions of people. Right now the promise changes depending on which slide you are on. That is the whole review: **make the product say one true thing, everywhere, and it is ready.**

The findings below are ordered by keynote risk. Each carries Location · Problem · Why it matters · Recommendation · Priority · Impact.

# TIER 0 — Keynote blockers

These are the issues that would visibly break on stage, contradict the narrator, or fail a first-row accessibility reviewer. None are hard to fix. All are non-negotiable.

## 0.1 — The core promise contradicts itself: one vs three vs four vs six

**Location.**
- `src/components/landing/analytics-demo.tsx:482` — lead eyebrow: **"One thing needs you today."**
- `src/components/marketing/briefing-anatomy.tsx:244` / `:264` — title **"Six things, in one short read."** and body **"The Daily Signal carries six signals."**
- `src/app/(marketing)/page.tsx:65` and `the-brief-hero` ledger — **"Three items per block, hard cap."**
- `src/app/(marketing)/signal/page.tsx:123` — **"Four blocks. Every time."**
- `src/app/(marketing)/method/page.tsx:82` — **"Three items per block. Always three."** vs `signal/page.tsx:13,26` — **"Up to three items."**

**Problem.** The single most important sentence in the entire product — how many things the briefing gives you — is stated as one, three, four, and six across the marketing funnel, and as "always three" vs "up to three" on two pages that link to each other. The homepage alone oscillates: the opener says three, the live demo one screen down says _one_, and the anatomy section below it says _six_.

**Why it matters.** This is the brand. "Three things in plain English" is the promise, the positioning, and the differentiator against dashboards. A visitor scrolling the homepage is told the product's fundamental shape three different ways in three consecutive sections. On a keynote slide, the narrator says "three things" while the screen says "six." It reads as a product that doesn't know itself — the exact opposite of the calm authority the design is reaching for. "Always three" is also simply _false_: the whole "silence is the signal" thesis requires "up to three."

**Recommendation.** Pick the canonical frame and enforce it everywhere. The honest model is: **up to three items per block; the morning brief renders two blocks (Needs attention, Quiet risks).** So the top-line promise is "up to three things that need you." Then: the demo's "One thing needs you today" is a _dynamic_ truth (some mornings there is one) — keep it, but never as a fixed brand claim. Kill "Six things, in one short read" as a headline — the anatomy diagram may show six _slots_, but the headline must not contradict the mandate. Change "Always three" → "Up to three" on `/method`. One number, one source of truth, propagated.

**Priority.** P0. **Impact.** Highest in the review. Fixing this single inconsistency does more for perceived quality than any visual polish.

## 0.2 — Dark mode renders two live surfaces as white slabs

**Location.** `src/components/marketing/briefing-anatomy.tsx:356` (`background: "#ffffff"` hardcoded on the demo card) · `src/components/account/danger-zone.tsx` (hardcoded `bg-white`, `rgba(255,255,255,0.70)` panel, rose `#9a3a3a` literals). Dark theme is fully defined in `src/ds/tokens.css:197–216`.

**Problem.** The design system ships a complete dark palette, but these surfaces bypass tokens and hardcode white backgrounds and near-black shadows. In dark mode the anatomy card — a centerpiece of the homepage — becomes a glaring white rectangle, and the account-deletion panel renders rose text on hardcoded white with unknown contrast.

**Why it matters.** Perceived quality is binary here: one white slab in an otherwise dark page reads as "broken," not "designed." The deletion flow is the highest-stakes screen in the product; it must not be the least theme-aware. And this is discoverable by any user whose OS is in dark mode.

**Recommendation.** Replace every hardcoded `#ffffff`, `rgba(255,255,255,…)`, and rose literal with the theme tokens (`--paper`, `--paper-elev`, `--ink`, and a tokenized `--danger`/rose ramp). Add dark-mode values for the danger ramp. The `.ds-grandfather.json` file already flags `danger-zone.tsx` (hex 7) and `briefing-anatomy.tsx` (hex 9) as debt — retire that debt.

**Priority.** P0. **Impact.** High. Removes the most visible "unfinished" signal in the product.

## 0.3 — The GDPR data export is an orphan: the API exists, the button does not

**Location.** `src/app/api/account/export/route.ts` (working GDPR Art. 20 JSON export) · `src/app/app/settings/account/page.tsx` (surfaces deletion only). No UI anywhere calls the export path.

**Problem.** The account page prominently surfaces the destructive path (delete) but offers no way to _export_ your data, even though a complete, auth-gated export endpoint is built and shipping. The right to portability exists only for someone who reads the source.

**Why it matters.** Trust and legal. "You will be able to export everything in a portable format before you leave" is an explicit promise on `/security`. Right now that promise is unkeepable through the UI. Surfacing "delete" without "export" is also the wrong emotional order — you offer the exit before the reassurance.

**Recommendation.** Add a quiet "Download your data" action on `/app/settings/account`, above the danger zone, wired to `/api/account/export`. One line of reassurance ("Everything Signal holds, as a JSON file"). It already works — it just needs a door.

**Priority.** P0. **Impact.** High. Closes a compliance gap and a trust gap at once.

## 0.4 — The typography demo autoplays, loops, and is invisible to assistive tech and search

**Location.** `src/app/(marketing)/demo/page.tsx` — `<video autoPlay muted loop playsInline controls>`.

**Problem.** The demo's entire content _is_ words — "typography only" — delivered as an MP4 with no captions/track, no transcript, no `prefers-reduced-motion` guard, and `objectFit:"cover"` (which can crop the words). None of the briefing copy exists as text in the DOM.

**Why it matters.** For a page whose whole purpose is showing sentences, a screen-reader or low-vision user gets _nothing_, and search engines index _nothing_. An autoplaying, looping motion piece with no reduced-motion path is also a textbook motion-sensitivity failure. This is the page you'd link from a keynote "watch the 30-second demo" — and it's the least accessible artifact in the product.

**Recommendation.** Render the demo's sentences as real, styled text in the DOM (progressively enhanced by the motion), or at minimum ship a visible transcript and a `<track kind="captions">`. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` (pause/first-frame). Do not `loop` an autoplay by default.

**Priority.** P0. **Impact.** High. Accessibility + SEO + the demo becomes reusable as text.

## 0.5 — Dead code ships the forbidden dashboards in the bundle

**Location.** `src/components/landing/analytics-hero-signal.tsx` (~830 lines, a **bar chart** with a scan beam and "↑ now") · `src/components/landing/analytics-hero-loader.tsx` (animates the stale word **"analytics"** and a "samples · 7" counter) · `src/components/marketing/briefing-compression.tsx` (a **node-graph** with a "RANKER" box and "RANK · CAP 3"). All exported, none imported.

**Problem.** Three large components — each a literal "dashboard chart" the brand explicitly refuses — still ship in the tree. Two carry stale branding ("analytics," "samples · 7") from before the product became "Signal."

**Why it matters.** They were correctly cut from the homepage, but leaving them in the bundle is dead weight and a live risk: the fastest way to violate the "no charts" mandate on stage is for someone to re-import the pretty bar-chart hero that's still sitting there compiling. It also confuses anyone reading the code about what the product _is_.

**Recommendation.** Delete all three. The mechanism diagrams that earn their place already live on `/law` and `/method`. If one is kept for reference, move it out of the shipped `src` tree.

**Priority.** P0 (hygiene, but do it before anyone builds a demo from this repo). **Impact.** Medium-high. Removes ~1,300 lines and the single biggest way to re-introduce a forbidden chart.

## 0.6 — "06:00 UTC" is shown to everyone, but onboarding promises the local morning

**Location.** `src/app/app/settings/notifications/cadence-form.tsx:19` — "Every morning at 06:00 UTC." · `src/app/app/onboarding/picker.tsx` — captures the browser IANA timezone. Corroborated by `voice.ts` (forEmail comment: cron fires 06:00 UTC) and CHANGELOG ("aging uses UTC day boundaries until the per-timezone cron exists").

**Problem.** Onboarding goes to visible trouble to detect and store your timezone ("Time zone: Europe/Dublin"), which sets the expectation that the briefing lands at _your_ 6am. But the cron fires at a fixed 06:00 UTC for everyone, and the settings copy says so. A user in California is told, at onboarding, that their timezone matters — then receives the brief at 10pm the night before.

**Why it matters.** It's a promise the product visibly makes and does not yet keep. For a product whose entire pitch is "before your day starts," delivering the brief in the middle of the previous evening is a credibility hit precisely on the core value prop.

**Recommendation.** Until the per-timezone cron exists, either (a) stop surfacing the captured timezone as if it drives send time, and make the "06:00 UTC" honest everywhere ("arrives 06:00 UTC — local send is coming"), or (b) ship the per-tz cron. Don't let onboarding imply a capability the sender doesn't have.

**Priority.** P0 (copy/expectation) / P1 (the feature). **Impact.** High on trust for non-EU users — i.e. most of a keynote audience.

## 0.7 — The briefing signs off, then a price tag speaks

**Location.** `src/app/app/brief/page.tsx:32–62` — the `<aside role="note">` "Your briefing is here. Get it in your inbox each morning, €12/month."

**Problem.** After the brief delivers its items and the grace note signs off ("That's the read."), a second voice appears and pitches €12/month with an underlined external link. This is the exact issue the 2026-06-09 review flagged (Gap 4); it was deferred to a founder gate and never resolved.

**Why it matters.** It is the most direct possible violation of "silence is the signal." The reader's last impression of the highest-stakes surface in the product is not the briefing — it's a price. On stage, the demo ends on an upsell card.

**Recommendation.** Remove the in-product paywall aside. The pricing page exists and is in the nav. If monetization must appear here, fold it into the existing grace-note register once (quiet mono, no card, no "€12"), not on every page load.

**Priority.** P0 for the keynote build; P1 otherwise (it's founder-gated per the prior review). **Impact.** High. Restores the ending the design earned.

## 0.8 — `/law` renders the one chart — and an equation — on a "no charts, plain English" product

**Location.** `src/app/(marketing)/law/page.tsx` — a scatter plot with axes ("work items (count)" × "attention-cost") and the equation `Signal = top₃( rank( triggers(work) ) ), where rank ⊥ model`.

**Problem.** The mandate is "no dashboard charts" and "plain sentences." This page is a data visualization plus function notation with subscripts and an orthogonality operator.

**Why it matters.** `/demo` and `/wedding-planning` both proudly say "No charts." `/law` renders one, and pairs it with the least-plain-English artifact in the product. It's defensible as a "Tufte-honest" argument page for the mechanism-curious, but it directly contradicts two stated refusals, and a skeptical reviewer will screenshot it next to "No charts."

**Why it matters (audience).** The target user is "someone running it themselves," not a mathematician. `top₃(rank(triggers(work)))` alienates exactly that person.

**Recommendation.** Keep `/law` as the mechanism-lover's page, but (a) reframe the scatter as an explicitly-labeled _diagram of a principle_, not a data chart, or replace it with the editorial "44 read · 41 set aside · 3 need you" tally that already works in the hero; and (b) demote the equation to a footnote or plain-English restatement. Don't let the product's own refusals page be refutable.

**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** Medium-high on brand coherence.

# TIER 1 — Major (per-surface)

## Trust & copy coherence (the marketing/content system)

### 1.1 — "Writes the briefing" vs "the engine never generates language"
**Location.** `about/page.tsx:74` ("a system that reads your work and **writes** the briefing"), `privacy/page.tsx:47` ("so the system can **write** the briefing"), `security/page.tsx` — vs `method/page.tsx` ("The engine never generates language. It picks language," "No machine writes these lines") and `changelog` ("Read-only, the engine cannot write").
**Problem.** The anti-AI positioning is the product's spine, and three trust pages describe the engine as _writing_ while the method/law pages insist it only _picks_ hand-written phrasings.
**Why it matters.** The one thing this product must be unimpeachable about is that no machine writes the words. Loose verbs on the trust pages hand a skeptic the crowbar.
**Recommendation.** Global find-and-replace on the generative verb. The engine _reads, selects, and assembles_ the briefing from a hand-written library. Never "writes."
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** High on the core differentiator.

### 1.2 — Anti-AI absolutism cracks between Method and Refusals
**Location.** `method/page.tsx` — "There is no model in the path" (absolute) vs `refusals/page.tsx` refusal 06 — "Even if a future cycle introduces an LLM somewhere in the pipeline…"
**Problem.** One page says never; the other pre-concedes an LLM is coming.
**Why it matters.** These two pages link to each other. The hedge undercuts the absolute, and a reader who visits both loses confidence in both.
**Recommendation.** Decide the true position and state it once. If an LLM may come but will never write user-facing language, say exactly that on both pages. If it's truly never, remove the hedge.
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** High on trust.

### 1.3 — Deletion timing contradiction
**Location.** `privacy/page.tsx` ("within 30 days") vs `security/page.tsx` ("immediate and permanent removal") — and the actual `DangerZone` deletes immediately.
**Problem.** Two published retention promises, and the shipped behavior matches one of them.
**Why it matters.** Data-deletion timing is a legal representation. Two numbers is a compliance liability, not a copy nit.
**Recommendation.** Make all three agree with the code: immediate and permanent. Fix the Privacy page.
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** High (legal/trust).

### 1.4 — Refusals have two sources of truth (four vs seven)
**Location.** `method/page.tsx` (four refusals) vs `refusals/page.tsx` (seven), both containing a differently-worded "Not a dashboard."
**Problem.** The product's public commitments exist in two lists that disagree in count and wording.
**Recommendation.** One canonical list (`/refusals`, sourced from PRODUCT.md); Method links to it rather than restating a subset.
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** Medium-high.

### 1.5 — Artifact naming and timing drift across the funnel
**Location.** Hero masthead "SIGNAL" / "Daily briefing · 06:00" (`analytics-demo`) / "Daily Signal · 09:14" (`briefing-anatomy`) / "COMPILED 8:42" (`the-brief-hero`). Program state: "private beta" (most pages) vs "private preview" (`waitlist`). Rule name: "Ten rules" (nav/hero) vs "ten triggers" (compression) vs "ten patterns" (method body).
**Problem.** The artifact has no canonical name (Signal? Daily Signal? Daily briefing?), no canonical send time (06:00 / 8:42 / 09:14), no canonical program-state word, and no canonical name for the ten things.
**Why it matters.** A keynote needs one noun and one time. Right now the same screen-to-screen scroll shows three of each. This is the "does this team talk to each other?" tell.
**Recommendation.** Lock four words in a glossary and enforce: **the product** = Signal; **the artifact** = the Daily Signal; **the send time** = one value; **the ten** = rules; **the program** = private beta. Propagate. (The mock times in demos should all read the same value.)
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** High on perceived cohesion.

### 1.6 — "Request access" points two different places and is sometimes not a link
**Location.** mailto on `signal`, `demo`, `pricing`, `waitlist`, `security`; but `/pricing` on `sign-in` and `sign-up`. Plain, non-clickable text on `privacy` and `terms`.
**Problem.** Same label, two destinations (and `/pricing` then bounces you to the mailto — an extra hop). Elsewhere the email is un-clickable text the user must copy.
**Recommendation.** One "Request access" behavior everywhere (a single mailto or a single form). Make every email a real `mailto:`.
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** Medium (conversion + polish).

### 1.7 — Internal/dev detail leaks onto public pages
**Location.** `refusals/page.tsx` footer exposes "docs/PRODUCT.md §7"; `changelog` leaks "icon.tsx and apple-icon.tsx," "11 public routes," "Content-Security-Policy (report-only)," "/signal says so"; `signal/page.tsx:270` shows orphan numerals "Daily 350. Weekly 900. Launch 1200." with no unit — ironic on a page that boasts "No counts."
**Problem.** The changelog reads like a developer's internal log wearing a marketing skin; the refusals page cites an internal file path; the cadence numbers are uninterpreted counts.
**Recommendation.** Rewrite changelog subs in user language; drop the internal path from `/refusals`; label or remove the "350/900/1200" numerals.
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** Medium.

## In-app, settings & forms

### 1.8 — Two "pick one" patterns, one of them accessibly wrong
**Location.** `onboarding/picker.tsx` uses a real `role="radiogroup"` + native radios (correct). `cadence-form.tsx` uses `<button aria-pressed>` styled as radios for a mutually-exclusive choice.
**Problem.** For a single-choice control, `aria-pressed` announces three independent toggles, not "1 of 3." The product builds the same pattern two different ways.
**Recommendation.** Make the cadence selector a real radiogroup (match the onboarding picker). One accessible pattern for "pick one," everywhere.
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** Medium-high (a11y correctness on a primary control).

### 1.9 — The cadence form reports success even when the save fails
**Location.** `cadence-form.tsx` `onSelect` — the value is set optimistically and `setSavedAt(Date.now())` fires unconditionally after the awaited action, with no `try/catch`.
**Problem.** If `updateCadenceAction` throws, the UI still flips to the new option and shows "Saved." The user believes their preference persisted when it didn't.
**Why it matters.** Silent write-failure on a preference the user explicitly set is a quiet trust breach — they'll stop (or keep) getting emails against their choice.
**Recommendation.** Wrap the action; on failure revert the optimistic value and show a quiet, live-region error ("Couldn't save — try again").
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** Medium-high.

### 1.10 — The account-deletion flow has accessibility and contrast gaps on the highest-stakes screen
**Location.** `danger-zone.tsx`. The confirm panel is a plain `<div>` (not `role="dialog"`/`alertdialog`), no focus trap, Escape doesn't cancel, the error `<p>` is not a live region, the disabled confirm button is `rgba(255,255,255,0.6)` text on `#e8c9c9` (contrast almost certainly fails), and `placeholder={email}` shows the exact string the user must type (undermining the deliberate-friction of a typed confirm), and the 429 rate-limit response isn't humanized (falls back to "Delete failed (429).").
**Problem.** The single most destructive, irreversible action has the weakest keyboard model, an unreadable disabled state, an unannounced error, and a confirmation whose "friction" is defeated by its own placeholder.
**Recommendation.** Promote the panel to `role="alertdialog"` with a focus trap and Escape-to-cancel; make the error an `aria-live` region; fix the disabled-button contrast via tokens; remove the email from the placeholder (use "your email address"); add a 429 → "Please wait a moment and try again" case to `humaniseError`.
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** High (a11y + trust on the deletion path).

### 1.11 — Keyboard focus is invisible on primary navigation and controls
**Location.** `suite-switcher-pills.tsx` (pills — only `:hover` themed, no `:focus-visible`), `chrome/suite-header.tsx` (text nav relies on UA default), `audience-toggle.tsx` (no focus ring), `danger-zone.tsx` trigger button.
**Problem.** Keyboard users cannot see where they are on the app's primary product switcher and on the marketing nav.
**Why it matters.** Focus visibility is a WCAG 2.4.7 baseline; on the chrome that appears on every page, its absence is systemic.
**Recommendation.** Add a single tokenized `:focus-visible` ring to the shared nav link/pill styles. (The design system already uses `outline: 2px solid var(--brand)` elsewhere — reuse it.)
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** Medium-high (applies everywhere).

### 1.12 — Global arrow keys fling users to another subdomain
**Location.** `suite-arrows.tsx` — window-level ArrowLeft/ArrowRight → `window.location.href` cross-product navigation, suppressed inside inputs but not for other focusable widgets.
**Problem.** A keyboard user arrowing through the cadence radios, the onboarding picker, or any non-input focusable can be navigated off to a different product subdomain with no warning.
**Why it matters.** Silent, unexpected cross-origin navigation on an arrow key is a serious wayfinding and accessibility hazard; there's also no visible hint the arrows do this.
**Recommendation.** Scope the shortcut behind an explicit affordance (e.g. only when the arrow control has focus, or a documented modifier), or remove the global key handler. At minimum, also suppress it when focus is inside any `role="radiogroup"`, listbox, or `[tabindex]` widget.
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** Medium-high.

### 1.13 — Reduced-motion users get an _incomplete_ briefing in the homepage demo
**Location.** `analytics-demo.tsx:188` — `if (reducedMotion) return` skips the entire timeline, so `delivered`/`whyThis`/phrasing never render.
**Problem.** With reduced motion on, the flagship demo never reaches its settled, informative state — no "Delivered," no "Why this?" — unlike `the-brief-hero`, which correctly settles to a complete SSR state.
**Why it matters.** Reduced-motion must remove motion, not content. These users see a truncated brief and miss the payoff.
**Recommendation.** On reduced motion, render the _final settled state_ (delivered, why-this available), just without the animation — mirror `the-brief-hero`'s pattern.
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** Medium-high (a11y parity on the headline demo).

### 1.14 — Two visible defects: a raw Clerk label and a broken CSS transition
**Location.** `user-button-with-suite.tsx:125` — `label="manageAccount"` may render the raw camelCase key in the user menu. `system/long-wait-status.tsx:51` — the `transition` string contains an inline `// ds-allow …` comment; `//` is invalid CSS and likely voids the whole declaration, so the long-wait line pops instead of fading.
**Problem.** One is a visible copy defect in the account menu; the other silently disables the one intentional animation on the long-wait status.
**Recommendation.** Verify/replace the Clerk action label with a proper "Manage account" string; move the `ds-allow` marker to a real CSS comment outside the value (or a code comment).
**Priority.** P1. **Impact.** Medium.

# TIER 2 — Refinements (still worth doing)

### 2.1 — The brief's loading skeleton promises a shape the brief doesn't have
**Location.** `app/brief/loading.tsx` renders items as **bordered, rounded cards**, but `briefing-view.tsx` renders items as **borderless list rows** with a left-accent hairline. The skeleton's section labels are sentence-case at `letter-spacing:-0.01em`; the real headings are **uppercase** at `tracking-[0.06em]`.
**Problem.** The skeleton's own "honesty contract" (only reserve what the surface renders) is violated by its item shape, and the label restyles (case + tracking) as it settles — a visible jump.
**Recommendation.** Match the skeleton to the real rows (borderless, left hairline) and render the labels uppercase so the settle is seamless.
**Priority.** P2. **Impact.** Medium (first-impression polish).

### 2.2 — The item feedback control is a sub-44px, focus-invisible tap target
**Location.** `briefing-view.tsx` `FeedbackControl` — "Useful? Yes / Not really" at 11.5px with no focus-visible ring and tiny hit areas.
**Problem.** The one interactive control on the brief is hard to hit on touch and invisible to keyboard focus.
**Recommendation.** Give the taps a real 44px target and a focus ring; keep the quiet look via padding, not shrinking.
**Priority.** P2. **Impact.** Medium.

### 2.3 — The "reader cursor" dim relies on hover and can disorient
**Location.** `briefing-view.tsx` `BriefRow` — hovering/focusing an item dims siblings to `opacity:0.45`.
**Problem.** Touch has no hover (so the affordance is desktop-only), and dimming the rest of the brief when a keyboard user merely _focuses_ a row can read as content disappearing.
**Recommendation.** Keep the active-item accent, but reconsider dimming the others on focus (or make the dim much shallower). Ensure the pattern degrades to "no change" on touch.
**Priority.** P2. **Impact.** Low-medium.

### 2.4 — Settings has no home and no connective tissue
**Location.** `/app/settings/account` and `/app/settings/notifications` exist; there is no `/app/settings` index and no in-page sub-nav between them (they're only reachable via the avatar menu).
**Problem.** A user on Notifications has no signal that Account exists; visiting `/app/settings` likely 404s to the _root_ (marketing) 404, which links to `/`, not back into the app.
**Recommendation.** Add a lightweight settings sub-nav (Notifications · Account) shared by both pages, and a `/app/settings` index that redirects to Notifications. Consider an app-scoped `not-found` that links to `/app/brief`.
**Priority.** P2. **Impact.** Medium (IA/discoverability).

### 2.5 — Error boundaries don't announce, and offer no reference
**Location.** `app/error.tsx`, `app/app/error.tsx` — no `role="alert"`/live region; digest is console-only; the two boundaries use slightly different reassurance strings.
**Problem.** Screen-reader users navigating into an error may hear nothing; users contacting support have no reference ID; the copy diverges between boundaries.
**Recommendation.** Add `role="alert"` to the error heading region; show the `digest` as a small "reference: …" line; unify the two reassurance strings.
**Priority.** P2. **Impact.** Low-medium.

### 2.6 — The homepage carries a duplicate CTA and two `<h1>`s
**Location.** The `Request access` + `Read a sample briefing` pair renders in both `the-brief-hero` (lines ~447) and the page-level `#cta`. The page has two `<h1>`s ("The signal, not the noise." and "Whatever you run, Signal reads it.").
**Problem.** The visitor sees the identical CTA twice; the document outline has two top-level headings.
**Recommendation.** Keep the closing CTA; let the hero's CTA be the hero's alone (or differentiate). Demote the second `<h1>` to `<h2>`.
**Priority.** P2. **Impact.** Low-medium (SEO + tightness).

### 2.7 — Mechanism jargon leaks into the marketing demo
**Location.** `why-this.tsx` renders `trigger · inactive-project · ≥ 8 days`, `slow-burn-deadline · ≤ 7 days, < 30% closed` (rule DSL with math operators); the `analytics-demo` sender chrome (avatar "S," "for · workspace," "Delivered to inbox" toast) markets an email-client mechanic.
**Problem.** The "Why this?" expansion exposes internal rule slugs and inequalities — the most "under-the-hood" moment on the marketing site — and the demo's inbox chrome sells email delivery over the briefing idea.
**Recommendation.** In "Why this?", render the plain-English reason only; drop the slug/operator caption (it lives on `/method` for those who want it). Soften the inbox chrome so the artifact reads as _the briefing_, not _an email_.
**Priority.** P2. **Impact.** Medium (brand register).

### 2.8 — Uncaptioned/unguarded decorative animations and small-print contrast
**Location.** Infinite loops not reduced-motion-guarded at component level: `tick-cursor.tsx` (pulse), `why-this.tsx` (caret). Lowest-contrast, smallest text recurs across surfaces: `--ink-quiet`/`--ink-faint` at 9.5–12px (hero ledger/"+38 more," anatomy intro, demo footer "Hard cap," `briefing-item` provenance at 10px, marketing eyebrows).
**Problem.** A few motion loops ignore the user preference; and the product's load-bearing promises ("Hard cap," "No model in the path") are frequently set in the faintest ink at the smallest sizes.
**Recommendation.** Guard the remaining infinite animations with `useReducedMotion`. Audit `--ink-quiet` (#71717a ≈ 4.6:1 on white — passes AA for normal text but is the floor) and never place load-bearing brand claims below `--ink-soft`; bump the 9.5–10.5px instances to ≥12px.
**Priority.** P2. **Impact.** Medium.

### 2.9 — The anatomy diagram has ~12 tab stops for 6 concepts, with invisible focus
**Location.** `briefing-anatomy.tsx` — each slot is interactive twice (a focusable `role="group"` card slot _and_ an annotation `<button>` with `outline:"none"` and `cursor:"default"`).
**Problem.** Keyboard users tab through twelve stops for six ideas, the buttons hide their focus ring, and the cursor says "not clickable" on things that are.
**Recommendation.** One interactive element per concept; restore a visible focus ring; use `cursor:pointer`.
**Priority.** P2. **Impact.** Medium.

# What is genuinely excellent — protect these

A subtraction pass must not become its own dashboard. These are the parts that already meet the bar:

- **The engine.** No model in the path, hard caps, deterministic hand-written prose. The hardest promise in the product is honored at the code layer. Never let a "smarter" idea erode it.
- **The all-clear / quiet-day surface** (`briefing-view.tsx` `AllClear`). One calm line, centered, the sampled signal-dot tick, no card. "Silence rendered as the achievement it is." This is the soul of the product — it's the slide to _open_ the keynote with, not close it.
- **The loading system.** The Layer-0 ready dot, the wordmark loader with genuine reduced-motion collapse, the honest brief skeleton (no fake counts), and the 5s long-wait escalation with a proper `role="status"`. A real philosophy, well executed.
- **`the-brief-hero`.** Editorial broadsheet, plain-English headlines, an honest "44 read · 41 set aside · 3 need you" tally, exemplary SSR-settled + reduced-motion handling. The best single argument the marketing makes.
- **The anti-AI copy that holds** — `Hero`'s "No model in the path. Written by hand." and the `CapOverflow` silent drop are the mandate made visible.
- **The notifications page voice** — "never a feed, never a dashboard," "If a day has no real signal, no email is sent," "we don't send empty briefings, by design." The strongest brand-consistent copy in the product.
- **Onboarding picker and audience toggle** — correct `radiogroup`, arrow-key roving tabindex, `aria-current`. The right accessible pattern, already built. Make everything else match _these_.
- **The unsubscribe design** (`/u/[token]`) — read-only GET so link-scanners can't auto-unsubscribe, CSRF-safe POST, plain-English states. A quietly excellent trust decision.

# The one STOP

**Stop shipping a product that describes itself differently on every screen.**

Everything in Tier 0 is a variant of one disease: the product has no single source of truth for its own promise. The count (one/three/four/six), the artifact's name (Signal / Daily Signal / Daily briefing), the send time (06:00 / 8:42 / 09:14), the program state (beta / preview), the deletion window (immediate / 30 days), and whether a machine writes the words — each is told more than one way. Individually they read as nits. Together they are the difference between a product that feels _authored_ and one that feels _assembled_.

Before any new feature, before any visual polish: write the one-page canon — **the noun, the number, the time, the verb, the promise** — and make every surface say exactly that. The engine is already honest. Make the words as disciplined as the math, and Signal is not just keynote-ready; it's the calmest, most self-assured thing on the slate.
