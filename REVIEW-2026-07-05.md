## How to read this document

precise about what I can see.

This session holds **one repository: Signal** (the attention-clarity product). Notes,
Tasks, and Timeline live in sibling repos I cannot read here. So the review has two
registers:

- **Signal** — reviewed at the code layer. Every claim below cites a file and line. This
  is a real excellence pass.
- **Notes / Tasks / Timeline** — reviewed at the **seam layer**: the positioning
  (`docs/PRODUCT.md`), the collaboration loop (`docs/COLLABORATION_LOOP.md`), the
  cross-product nav (`suite-arrows.tsx`), and the read contract Signal has with Tasks
  (`PRODUCT.md §6`). I answer the twelve questions for each, but I flag where a

A prior pass, `REVIEW-2026-06-09.md`, made five cuts. Four shipped (A·3–A·5). This review
starts by checking whether they actually landed, then goes wider.

## The one thing — the shop window still sells a product we stopped shipping

If only one line ships from this review, ship this.

**The brief the customer *sees before signing up* is not the brief we *deliver after they
sign in*.** The June pass cut two of the four blocks from the morning read:

- Shipped brief — `briefing-view.tsx:106–128` renders **two blocks**: Needs attention,
  Quiet risks. "Moving well" and "Suggested focus" are gone, with a load-bearing comment
  explaining why (the engine weighted `just-shipped` at 100/1000, lowest of six; Suggested
  focus was a re-projection of items already on the page).

But the two surfaces a prospect actually meets still promise **four**:

- The interactive homepage demo — `domains.ts` defines all four blocks for every audience
  pack, and `analytics-demo.tsx:320` renders `pack.blocks` wholesale.
- The "Read a sample briefing" page — `/wedding-planning/page.tsx:11–47` hand-codes all
  four, Moving well and Suggested focus included.

So a wedding planner watches a four-block demo, clicks "Read a sample," reads a four-block
sample, requests access, signs in — and receives a two-block brief. The product got
sharper and the shop window did not follow. This is precisely the failure `PRODUCT.md §12`
was written to forbid: *"When the live marketing site says one thing and this document
says another, one of them is wrong. The fix is not to leave them inconsistent."*

The June review couldn't catch this — the cuts hadn't shipped yet when it was written. Now
they have, and the demo is the thing over-promising. It is noise dressed as signal, on the
highest-intent surface in the funnel.

**Fix:** resync the demo and the sample to the two-block brief. Keep the audience toggle,
the "Why this" expansion, the mid-loop phrasing swap — all of that is the demo at its best.
Cut the Moving-well and Suggested-focus columns so the demo *is* the product. This is
founder-adjacent (the demo features the buckets prominently; see June STOP), so it ships on
Ethan's nod — but the recommendation is unambiguous.

## Product 1 · Signal — the attention-clarity product

**Read at the code layer.** `src/lib/briefing/*`, `src/components/brief/*`,
`src/app/app/brief/*`, `src/app/(marketing)/*`.

### Who is this for?
The person the work routes through who has no project manager and does not work in tech: the
wedding planner running six weddings, the trades operator with crews on four sites, the
freelancer with eleven clients, the final-year student running research and teaching in
parallel (`PRODUCT.md §2`; the four demo packs in `domains.ts` are exactly these). The
audience is well-drawn and *disciplined* — the banned framings ("engineering teams",
"stakeholders") are honoured throughout the copy. This is a strength; hold it.

### What problem does it solve?
"What needs my attention today, before it becomes a problem?" — answered as a two-minute
read of plain sentences instead of a board to check. The briefing replaces the dashboard.

### What is confusing?
1. **The demo/product mismatch above.** The single biggest source of confusion is a
   promise the product then contradicts.
2. **"Suggested focus" still exists in three places at once** — cut from the web brief,
   still built by the engine (`build.ts:108–110, 136–138`), still shown in the demo and
   sample. A reader who compares surfaces can't tell whether it's a feature or not. Pick one
   answer and make every surface say it.
3. **The empty-state escape hatch points off-property.** `AllClear` (`briefing-view.tsx:505`)
   links to `tasks.signalstudio.ie/app`. Correct destination, but it's the only place the
   quiet-day surface sends you, and it leaves Signal entirely. Fine — just intentional.

### What should be removed?
- **The in-app paywall card** (`app/brief/page.tsx:32–62`). This is the last unshipped June
  finding, knowingly deferred as founder-gated (A·3 changelog). It is a dashboard reflex:
  the brief signs off with `graceNote()` ("That's the read.") and then a second voice cuts
  in to pitch €12/month with an underlined external link. The reader's last impression of
  the briefing is a price tag. It violates "silence is the signal" more directly than
  anything else on the surface. **Recommend removal;** fallback is to fold it into the
  grace-note register, once, in quiet mono — no card, no border, no `€12/month` in body
  copy. Founder-gated on pricing copy.
- **`movingWell` / `suggestedFocus` from the engine's *default* build** — or, cleaner, keep
  them but document loudly that they are email/future-surface-only. Right now the engine
  computes four blocks and the web throws two away; that's fine for reuse but it's a trap
  for the next engineer who wires a new surface and accidentally re-ships the cut blocks
  (the demo already did exactly this).

### What should be simplified?
The **email vs web block story**. Today the web renders two blocks and the email render path
still has access to four. Before that divergence hardens, decide the canonical brief shape
*once* (`PRODUCT.md §4` still says "Always four") and make PRODUCT.md, the engine, the web,
the email, the demo, and the sample all agree. This is a contract-alignment task, not a
build task, and it's overdue.

### What should be delayed until later?
- **Per-timezone cron.** Aging and dispatch use UTC day boundaries (A·5 honest edge). Real
  friction, but it's a day-two problem — defer until there are enough non-EU users to feel
  it.
- **PRODUCT.md §5.3 day-three suppression.** Correctly *not* built yet (A·5) — auto-hiding
  what a reader chose to keep needs its own pass now that the dismiss control exists. Hold.
- **External sources** (Calendar, Slack, email reads). `PRODUCT.md §6` defers these
  explicitly. Do not let a prospect conversation pull them forward; Tasks-as-data-layer is
  the right v1 scope.

### What should be redesigned?
Nothing structural. The brief render is close to right. The one redesign worth doing is the
**demo**, and it's a subtraction, not a redesign — see "The one thing."

### What moments create delight?
- **The all-clear / quiet-day page** (`briefing-view.tsx:438–516`). A designed *destination*,
  not a fallback — display type, the signal dot ticking its sampled cadence, no gray box.
  A product that makes *nothing to report* feel like an achievement is a product with a
  point of view. This is the best screen in the suite. (A·5.)
- **"Why this" expansion** — the determinism made experienceable (`WhyThisAccordion`). The
  reader can always answer "why did I see this?" in one sentence. This is the moat.
- **`name-the-blocker` phrasing** — "blocked by Music supplier for 9 days" (`prose/*`). The
  briefing at its most specific and human. The bar for any new trigger.

### What moments create friction?
- The paywall card (above).
- The demo→product gap (above) — the friction lands *after* activation, which is the worst
  place for it: it's a trust tax paid on day one.
- **First-day / no-history behavior** is still an open question (`PRODUCT.md §11 Q3`). A new
  user with four tasks and no history is the make-or-break first impression, and we haven't
  locked what they see. This is a bigger activation risk than any polish item here.

### What would increase retention?
The briefing has to be *right* more than it has to be *pretty*, and it's already pretty. The
feedback loop (A·4/A·5: "Not really" → engine suppresses that trigger for that item) is the
correct retention mechanism because it makes the brief get more trustworthy with use.
**Invest there:** surface, quietly, that the brief is learning from the reader's taps
("Showing less of X since you skipped it") — not as a dashboard, as a single honest line.
Trust compounds; that's retention.

### What would increase activation?
Lock the **day-one brief** (`PRODUCT.md §11 Q3`). Time-to-first-briefing under five minutes
is a stated v1 gate (`§8`); a new user with thin data needs a first brief that feels earned,
not empty and not fabricated. This is the highest-leverage unshipped activation work.

### What would make someone recommend this product?
The all-clear page and the "Why this" honesty. People recommend products that make them feel
*calm* and *in control*, and Signal's two best moments do exactly that. The thing that would
make them recommend it *more*: never being surprised by it. Which routes straight back to
closing the demo→product gap — you recommend what does what it said it would.

## Product 2 · Tasks — the execution layer

**Read at the seam layer.** Tasks is Signal's sole data source (`PRODUCT.md §6`;
`src/server/tasks-db/*`, `src/lib/briefing/tasks-db-source.ts`). Code-level review owed in
the Tasks repo.

- **Who / problem:** the same no-PM operator, capturing work at "three-second-capture speed"
  (`PRODUCT.md §6`). Tasks is where work is *entered and moved*; Signal is where it's *read*.
- **What's confusing (from Signal's seam):** the **tag-as-project** model. Signal derives a
  "project" from each unique tag (`PRODUCT.md §6`). This is the right anti-configuration
  call, but it means a mistyped or synonymous tag ("claire-wedding" vs "claire_wedding")
  silently splits one project into two, and Signal's briefing will read them as two quiet
  projects instead of one. **The friction is invisible in Tasks and only surfaces in
  Signal.** Worth a light-touch tag-hygiene nudge in Tasks (dedupe/merge suggestion), owned
  by Tasks, motivated by Signal.
- **What to simplify / not build:** resist adding a `projects` table to Tasks — `PRODUCT.md
  §6` argues this correctly (it would reintroduce a configuration tax). Hold that line.
- **Delight / friction:** the lane vocabulary (`todo/doing/review/done`) canonicalises
  cleanly into Signal's status enum (`PRODUCT.md §6`). The calm rename (Waiting vs Blocked,
  A·2, mirrored from Tasks T·81) is the suite acting as one product. That's a delight moment
  most users won't consciously notice — which is the point.
- **Retention / activation / recommend:** Tasks is the activation *engine* for the whole
  suite — no tasks, no signal, no roadmap read. Its capture speed is the top of every funnel.
  The single highest-leverage suite investment is making Tasks capture frictionless, because
  every downstream product inherits its emptiness or richness.

**Owed:** a code-layer pass in the Tasks repo on capture latency, tag hygiene, and the
lane→status contract at the source.

## Product 3 · Timeline (Roadmap) — the direction layer

**Read at the seam layer.** `suite-arrows.tsx` names it `roadmap`/"timeline"; `PRODUCT.md §1`
positions it as *direction* clarity ("explains the work"). Code-level review owed in the
Roadmap repo.

- **Who / problem:** the operator asking "are we heading the right way?" vs Signal's "what
  needs me today?" The split is clean and worth protecting.
- **What's confusing:** the **Timeline / Signal boundary** is the suite's most confusable
  seam. `PRODUCT.md §6` deliberately keeps Timeline data *out* of the daily brief ("mixing
  its data into the daily briefing would conflate the two"). Good discipline — but a user
  won't know that rule exists. The risk is they expect "roadmap confidence changed" to show
  up in tomorrow's brief and it doesn't. The `COLLABORATION_LOOP.md` even lists "roadmap
  confidence changed" as a Signal event, so the boundary is genuinely fuzzy in the docs.
  **Resolve it:** decide once whether roadmap-direction signals belong in Signal's *weekly*
  cadence (defensible) and never the daily, then say so in both products.
- **What to delay / not build:** do not let Timeline grow daily-standup features — that's
  Signal's job. Timeline is the ten-thousand-foot view; keep its cadence slow.
- **Recommend:** the suite story ("capture → execute → plan → measure",
  `suite-arrows.tsx:14`) is what makes any one product recommendable — people recommend the
  *loop*, not the node.

**Owed:** a code-layer pass in the Roadmap repo, especially on where direction-confidence
signals are meant to surface.

## Product 4 · Notes — the capture layer

**Read at the seam layer.** `suite-arrows.tsx` places `notes` first in the loop ("capture");
`COLLABORATION_LOOP.md` lists Note as "context input for unresolved actions, risks,
questions, and decisions." Code-level review owed in the Notes repo.

- **Who / problem:** the operator dumping raw context — the meeting, the phone call, the
  half-formed decision — before it's structured into tasks. Notes is the mouth of the funnel.
- **What's confusing (from the seam):** Notes' relationship to Signal is the **least defined
  in the suite.** Signal reads Tasks, not Notes (`PRODUCT.md §6` read model is tasks +
  projects + activity events; notes appear nowhere). Yet `COLLABORATION_LOOP.md` says Notes
  carries "unresolved actions, risks, questions, decisions" — exactly the raw material a
  briefing wants. So there's a live question: **should Signal ever read Notes?** Today: no,
  and correctly so for v1 (it would multiply surface area). But it's the most interesting
  *future* seam in the suite and should be named as a deliberate v2+ question, not left
  ambient.
- **What to delay:** Notes→Signal reads. Tempting, scope-exploding, defer past v1.
- **Recommend / retention:** Notes is where the habit starts. If capture isn't effortless,
  nothing downstream fills. Its activation *is* the suite's activation.

**Owed:** a code-layer pass in the Notes repo; and a suite decision on whether Notes ever
becomes a Signal source.

## The suite seam — what only this vantage shows

Reviewing the suite *through Signal* surfaces three cross-product truths a single-product
review would miss:

1. **The loop is the product; the nodes are features.** `capture → execute → plan → measure`
   (`suite-arrows.tsx`). Every "recommend this product" answer above collapsed to "recommend
   the loop." The suite's job is to make the *handoffs* invisible — a task captured in Notes,
   moved in Tasks, planned in Timeline, and read in Signal without the user ever feeling four
   products. The calm-vocabulary alignment (A·2: Waiting/Moving shared across all four) is the
   suite doing this well. Protect it: any product that drifts its vocabulary breaks the
   single-product illusion.

2. **Signal is the loop's proof.** `COLLABORATION_LOOP.md` names Signal the owner of "work
   becomes clearer" and "shareable briefing." Signal is where a user *feels* the payoff of
   having used the other three. That makes Signal's trustworthiness the suite's retention
   lever — which is why the demo→product gap (§"The one thing") is a *suite* problem, not a
   Signal problem. A brief that over-promises undermines the reason to keep feeding the loop.

3. **Configuration is the shared enemy.** Every product refuses configuration
   (`PRODUCT.md §3`, §6, §7). Tag-as-project, no-projects-table, no rule editor, no
   threshold sliders. This is the suite's spine. The single most damaging thing any product
   could ship is a settings page that makes the user do the software's job.

## Roadmap

Ordered by leverage, not by size. Focus first; features never.

### Immediate (this cycle — subtraction and alignment only)
1. **Resync the demo + sample to the two-block brief.** Cut Moving-well and Suggested-focus
   columns from `analytics-demo.tsx` and `/wedding-planning`. Close the demo→product gap.
   *(Founder-adjacent; recommendation unambiguous.)*
2. **Remove the in-app paywall card** (`app/brief/page.tsx:32–62`), or fold it into the
   grace-note register once. *(Founder-gated — pricing copy.)*
3. **Reconcile `PRODUCT.md §4` ("Always four") with the shipped two-block brief.** The
   contract still says four; the product ships two. Fix the contract, then confirm engine,
   web, email, demo, sample all agree. Pure alignment.

### Version 1.1 (make the brief trustworthy, not bigger)
4. **Lock the day-one brief** (`PRODUCT.md §11 Q3`). The thin-data first impression is the
   top activation risk. Ship a considered first-run brief that feels earned.
5. **Surface the learning loop, quietly.** One honest line when the "Not really" feedback has
   changed what shows ("Showing less of X"). Retention through trust, not through a dashboard.
6. **Name the Timeline↔Signal and Notes↔Signal boundaries** in `PRODUCT.md` and
   `COLLABORATION_LOOP.md`. Resolve where roadmap-confidence signals belong (weekly, never
   daily) and whether Notes ever becomes a source (v2+ question, stated not ambient).

### Version 2 (extend the read, hold the discipline)
7. **Weekly cadence as a real surface.** `PRODUCT.md §4` promises three cadences; the daily is
   the shipped one. The weekly (ten-minute Friday read, trend lines *in sentences*) is where
   "Moving well" and direction signals could honestly live — off the daily critical path.
8. **Tag hygiene nudge in Tasks** (owned by Tasks, motivated by Signal): dedupe/merge
   near-identical tags so Signal doesn't read one project as two.
9. **Per-timezone cron.** Retire the UTC-day honest-edge once non-EU usage justifies it.

### Version 3 (new surface area — only after the loop is proven)
10. **First external source** (`PRODUCT.md §6` deferred list). One, chosen by real demand —
    most likely Calendar, since deadlines are the briefing's strongest signal. Not Slack, not
    email, not all-at-once.
11. **Notes as a Signal source** — the suite's most interesting future seam. Only if v1/v2
    prove the loop and only with the same anti-configuration discipline.

## What I am explicitly NOT recommending (feature-creep firebreak)

So subtraction doesn't quietly become its own dashboard:

- **No metric view, no counters, no score.** The recurring failure mode (`PRODUCT.md §8`).
  If a user asks for a dashboard, the brief failed to be sufficient — the fix is a better
  brief, not a dashboard.
- **No LLM in the briefing path for v1** (`PRODUCT.md §9`, locked). The "no model in the
  path" claim is the brand's hardest-won promise and it's true at the code layer. Do not let
  a "smarter phrasing" request erode it. Revisit only on the defined triggers (§9), not on a
  calendar date.
- **No per-seat pricing, no usage tiers** (`pricing/page.tsx:101–108`). One plan, one price.
  Hold.
- **No roles/permissions/SSO/audit logs at v1** (`PRODUCT.md §7`). Defer to demand.
- **No real-time / notification stream.** Fixed cadence is the product. A live view means the
  user wants Tasks, not Signal.

## Order of operations

If one thing ships: **resync the demo to the two-block brief** (Immediate #1). It's the gap
between what we promise and what we deliver, on the highest-intent surface, and it's a pure
subtraction.

If two: add **remove the paywall card** (Immediate #2) — the last dashboard reflex on the
product's proudest surface.

If three: **reconcile the PRODUCT.md contract** (Immediate #3), so the next engineer can't
accidentally re-ship the cut blocks the way the demo did.

Those three land the promise, the product, and the contract on the same shape: a briefing,
two honest blocks, nothing that talks after it has finished talking. The mandate, made
literal.
