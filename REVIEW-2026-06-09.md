_Director of Analytics Product Excellence — Layer 2 (Recommend)_

# Signal Analytics / Daily Signal — Excellence Review

**Date:** 2026-06-09
**Scope:** the shipped product surface — `/app/brief`, the marketing homepage, the briefing engine output. Read-only pass.
**Bar:** "a briefing, not a dashboard. Three things in plain English. Silence is the signal." (BRAND.md §2, hq/products/analytics.md, PRODUCT.md §4–§5.)

The product is close. The engine has been disciplined — six triggers, hard caps, rotation, no LLM in the path. The drift is not in the math. It is in the surfaces that wrap the math. The brief has grown a fourth bucket that duplicates the first two. The marketing page has grown three diagrams that explain the mechanism instead of letting the mechanism speak. The in-app surface has grown a paywall card that talks when the brief has stopped talking. Each of these is a small dashboard re-entering through a different door.

Five gaps follow. Then one stop.

---

## Gap 1 — The brief now ships four buckets, not three. "Suggested focus" duplicates "Needs attention".

**Surface.** `/app/brief` · `src/components/brief/briefing-view.tsx:92–110` (the `Bucket` × 3 + `FocusBlock` render tree) · engine: `src/lib/briefing/build.ts:82–98` and `:146–175`.

**Reads as.** Dashboard-shape. The page renders up to **12 items across 4 sections** (3 × Needs attention + 3 × Moving well + 3 × Quiet risks + 3 × Suggested focus). The mandate is *three things in plain English*. Twelve is not three. The hero on the marketing surface even says it: "Three things per block, hard cap. The signal, not the noise." (`hero.tsx:120`). The product ships four blocks.

**Worse, "Suggested focus" is mathematically a re-projection of the other buckets.** From `build.ts:82–85`:

```ts
const focusSource = [...attention, ...risks]
  .sort((a, b) => focusWeight(b) - focusWeight(a))
  .slice(0, BUCKET_CAP);
```

The focus block is a **sorted top-3 of items the reader already read above**. Same task, same title, second time on screen, with a different label and a small calendar chip. A reader on a busy day sees "Florist deposit has been blocked by Music supplier for 9 days" in Quiet risks, then sees the same task again in Suggested focus as "Florist deposit · this week". That is the definition of a dashboard tic: re-rendering the same data under another header. The "Suggested focus" label was meant to be the strongest verb the briefing uses (`build.ts:161–166` comment); right now it is the most repetitive one.

**Cut.** One of two cuts, in order of preference.

1. **Preferred — cut "Suggested focus" entirely.** The three Needs-attention items are already the focus. The block adds a header and re-renders the same titles below. Removing it returns the brief to the mandate count: three buckets, three items each, max nine. The block-order array at `compression/types.ts` and `build.ts` collapses by one. The hero's "three items per block" claim becomes load-bearing instead of decorative.
2. **Fallback — keep Suggested focus, cut "Needs attention".** Make Focus the lead block. Reader sees the action first, the context second (Moving well, Quiet risks). This is the more conservative trim but it inverts the brief's current shape and is the larger build.

The math in `focusWeight` (`build.ts:204–211`) stays useful either way — `due-soon 1000 / crowded-week 800 / stuck-work 700 / blocked-too-long 600 / overload 500` becomes the **sort order inside Needs-attention**, not a fourth bucket.

**Layer.** 2 — Recommend. This is a metric-cut. The Director's mandate (charter §Decision boundaries) covers it. Founder-gate only if the cut changes pricing copy ("Suggested focus" is named in marketing).

## Gap 2 — The `/app/brief` header carries two timestamps. One of them is a chip that says nothing.

**Surface.** `src/components/brief/briefing-view.tsx:136–156` — `Header({ stamp })`.

**Reads as.** Dashboard chrome. The header renders `Daily Signal · Monday 09:14` on the left and a mono `Today` chip on the right. The chip is decoration. It says nothing the date string did not just say, and it is the kind of widget that lives on a Tableau page, not on a morning read. It was the right shape when there was a Today/Yesterday toggle planned; the Yesterday toggle was cut on 2026-05-15 (hq/products/analytics.md A·7 entry) and the `Today` chip never went with it.

**Cut.** Delete the right-hand `<p>Today</p>` at `briefing-view.tsx:147–153`. The left-hand stamp carries the full meaning. Saves a JSX element, a tracking constant, and one dashboard-shaped piece of furniture on the highest-stakes surface in the product.

**Layer.** 1 — Decide. Pure subtraction. Ship it.

## Gap 3 — The marketing surface explains the mechanism three times. Each explanation is a small dashboard.

**Surface.** `src/app/(marketing)/page.tsx:18–73` — the homepage composition:

1. `AnalyticsHeroSignal` — the scan-line bar chart hero (`analytics-hero-signal.tsx`, 893 lines).
2. `Hero` — eyebrow + H1 + rotating subhead + status pip + audience toggle + live `AnalyticsDemo` (the actual product speaking for itself).
3. **Standalone interstitial** — "The engine never generates language. It picks language." (`page.tsx:25–64`).
4. `BriefingCompression` — SVG diagram (`briefing-compression.tsx:91–267`): three columns, ten trigger labels on the left, a `RANK · CAP 3 ·` pill in the middle, three surfaced items on the right, fan-in lines between them.
5. `BriefingAnatomy` — `briefing-anatomy.tsx`, **859 lines**, six numbered slots, choreographed cap-fill bar, focus pip, why-this expansion.
6. CTA.

That is **three separate mechanism diagrams** (Compression, Anatomy, the rotating-subhead Hero) for a product whose mandate is *don't show the mechanism, show the briefing*. A reader who came for "what would land in my inbox tomorrow morning" walks through "what picking looks like", a ranker pill, a cap bar ticking 0→3, and a fan-in SVG before reaching the CTA. Compare to BRAND.md §2.2 fail mode #3 — "Surface bloat. The user reads this as 'this is not for me; this is for someone whose job is using software.'"

**Quantified drift.** The marketing page now carries roughly **2,000 lines of mechanism-explanation TSX** (`analytics-hero-signal` 893 + `briefing-anatomy` 859 + `briefing-compression` 286 + the interstitial copy block). The actual briefing render (`briefing-view.tsx`) is **461 lines**. The product is **outweighed 4:1 on its own homepage by diagrams of how it works.**

**Cut.** Keep one of the three diagrams. The right one is **BriefingAnatomy** — it is the only one that shows the artifact the customer will receive, in the customer's vocabulary, with the customer's brief shape. **Cut `BriefingCompression`** (the 10→3 fan-in SVG) — it teaches the engine, not the brief. **Cut the standalone "engine never generates language" interstitial** at `page.tsx:25–64` — that line belongs on `/law` or `/method`, where the readers who care about mechanism go on purpose. Both /law and /method already exist (`src/app/(marketing)/{law,method}/page.tsx`); the diagrams have a home that earned them.

The page then reads: Hero (with live demo) → Anatomy → CTA. Three beats. Same shape as the brief itself.

**Layer.** 2 — Recommend. This is a surface-area cut on the highest-traffic marketing page. Surface to Ethan as a brief; do not ship under the Director's own authority alone (charter §Decision boundaries — "material" surface change).

## Gap 4 — The in-app brief speaks when it has finished speaking. The paywall card is a dashboard reflex.

**Surface.** `src/app/app/brief/page.tsx:32–62` — the `<aside role="note">` rendered below the brief for users below the `workspace` tier.

**Reads as.** A widget that fires *after* the brief has said its piece. The card carries:

- Body line: "Your briefing is here."
- Marketing line: "Get it in your inbox each morning — €12/month."
- Underlined `https://signalstudio.ie/pricing` link in `target="_blank"`.

This violates *silence is the signal* in the most direct way the surface allows. The brief just delivered three (well, twelve — see Gap 1) plain-English items. The grace note at the bottom (`graceNote()` in `voice.ts:61–66`) has already signed off — "That's the read." or "Take the focus block first. The rest can wait." Then a second voice cuts in and pitches €12/month. The reader's last impression of the briefing surface is not the brief. It is a price tag.

**Cut.** Two options.

1. **Preferred — remove the in-product paywall card.** The pricing page exists. The route is in the nav. A user who wants the inbox version will find `signalstudio.ie/pricing` without an asideroleequalsnote pushing them. Trust the surface.
2. **Fallback — move the upgrade message into the existing grace-note voice and ship it once per week, not every page-load.** "Inbox delivery is on the workspace plan." rendered in `ink-quiet` mono at the foot, no border, no card, no `€12/month` in body copy. The grace-note pattern (`voice.ts:61–66`) is built for exactly this register.

**Layer.** 3 — Founder-gate. Pricing copy on the product surface is founder-gated per the charter ("Pricing changes for Analytics are founder-gated"). Director recommends, founder approves.

## Gap 5 — `summaryLine()` speaks on quiet days. The function exists to fill a slot.

**Surface.** `src/lib/briefing/voice.ts:41–55` — `summaryLine(b: Briefing): string`.

**Reads as.** A line that fires every morning, including the morning when there is nothing to fire about. From `voice.ts:45–47`:

```ts
if (att === 0 && risks === 0) {
  if (moving > 0) return "Light day. The board is moving.";
  return "All quiet. Nothing pulling.";
}
```

Compare to BRAND.md §6.5 reference example, the hypothetical `A·09` dispatch — "if nothing in your workspace moved yesterday, the briefing doesn't arrive." The cron already implements that refusal at dispatch time (`hq/products/analytics.md` Phase C). But the **web view** at `/app/brief` will still render `summaryLine()` on a quiet day, with a generated sentence ("Light day. The board is moving.") that is structurally a filler — the function exists because a slot exists.

"Nothing pulling" is honest. "Light day. The board is moving." is the kind of cheerful one-liner that surfaces grow when they cannot bear a blank line. It is small, but it is the silhouette of dashboard-voice — the system speaking because it is on, not because it has something to say.

**Cut.** Remove the `att === 0 && risks === 0` branch. On those days, render `summaryLine()` as the empty string and let the `EmptyState` component (already wired at `briefing-view.tsx:89–91`) carry the whole frame. The empty state's "Nothing to flag today." is the right voice for the empty case. The summary line should fire only when there is something to summarise.

**Layer.** 1 — Decide. Voice subtraction inside the existing voice module. Ship it.

## STOP — Stop shipping "Moving well" as a default block.

**Surface.** `briefing-view.tsx:98–103`; `build.ts:55–62` (the bucket); `briefing-anatomy.tsx:57–60` (the marketing slot annotation: *"Quiet wins worth knowing. Calibrates against the noise of what's wrong."*).

**Why this violates the mandate.** "Moving well" is the celebration bucket. It exists to render `just-shipped` items — and `just-shipped` has the lowest possible focus weight in the engine (`build.ts:209`: `"just-shipped": 100`, sixth out of six triggers). The engine has already decided this bucket is not the signal. The render layer ships it anyway because there is a slot for it.

A daily celebration of shipped work is the inverse of *silence is the signal*. On a quiet morning the brief should be quiet. On a busy morning, three "moving well" items push three "quiet risks" items further down the page — celebration crowding out the part of the brief the reader actually needed to see. The bucket's own copy at `briefing-anatomy.tsx:59` argues for itself by saying it "calibrates against the noise of what's wrong" — calibration is a dashboard verb, not a briefing verb.

**Stop.** Cut the "Moving well" block from the default render. The `just-shipped` trigger stays in the engine (the math is correct and may earn other uses — a weekly cadence, a silent reward signal, an undo prompt). The default morning brief renders Needs-attention + Quiet-risks only. If a user explicitly wants a "what shipped" view, it lives on a separate surface, not in the morning read.

This is the single largest single move toward the mandate. It is also the easiest to ship — one block removal, one BLOCK_ORDER constant.

**Layer.** 2 — Recommend, escalating to founder-gate because the demo and the marketing page both feature the bucket prominently.

## What stays — the things this review found right

For the record, so subtraction does not become its own dashboard:

- **The engine.** Six triggers, hard caps, deterministic prose rotation, no LLM in the path (`build.ts`, `triggers/index.ts`, `prose/index.ts`). The brand's hardest promise (PRODUCT.md §5.2) is honoured at the code layer. Do not let any "smarter" suggestion erode this.
- **The cron refusal on empty days.** `dispatchBriefing()` refuses to send when the brief is empty (`hq/products/analytics.md` Phase C, Suite Review Pass 2026-05-13). This is the brand position made operational. Hold.
- **The voice module.** `voice.ts:20–35` — single source of truth across HTML email, plain text, and web. `forEmail` flag handles the UTC-cron timezone problem honestly. Hold.
- **`name-the-blocker` phrasing.** "blocked by Music supplier for 9 days" (Phase F.2) is the brief at its best — specific, plain-English, attributable. The bar for any new trigger is *this kind of sentence or do not ship it.*
- **The loading boundary fix (2026-05-19).** Closing the 3.5s naked-dot void was a real first-impression repair. Held.

## Order of operations

If only one item ships from this review, ship the STOP (cut "Moving well"). It is the single largest signal-to-noise move available and the cheapest to make.

If two, add Gap 1 (cut "Suggested focus" or invert it with Needs attention).

If three, add Gap 5 (remove the quiet-day filler line). These three together would land the brief at three buckets, three items, three honest sentences. The mandate, finally and literally.

Marketing-surface cuts (Gap 3) and pricing copy (Gap 4) carry founder weight and ship on a different track.
